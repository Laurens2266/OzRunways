Radio Calls — Coldstream & Other Non-Towered Aerodromes

Aircraft

VH-MSG · Piper Warrior I · Callsign: "Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf"

Radio calls consist of discrete parts (in this order):

  1. Who you are addressing.
  2. Who you are – aircraft type and callsign.
  3. Where you are – ground position.
  4. Your altitude.
  5. Your intentions.
  6. Your operating location (just "Coldstream" not "Coldstream traffic").

Taxiing (expected*)

Coldstream traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf, Taxiing for (destination or intention) circuits/training area, (runway) one seven/three five Coldstream.

Entering the runway (expected*)

Coldstream traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf Entering and rolling (runway) one seven for circuits Coldstream.
Note: We normally enter the runway to "roll" — this refers to the take-off roll. But we could also enter the runway to back-track (use it as a taxiway), or to hold because we are not yet ready to take off.

Outbound (Coldstream requirement)

Coldstream traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf, (position) over the top/two miles south (level) two thousand, (intentions) tracking south west, from Coldstream.

Inbound (expected*)

Yarra Valley traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf (position) one zero south west, (level) two thousand, (intentions) inbound Coldstream.

About to join circuit (expected*)

Coldstream traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf, (position) three miles south/over the top, (level) one thousand five hundred, (intentions) joining downwind/upwind/crosswind (runway) one seven Coldstream.

Base turn (Coldstream requirement)

Downwind and final calls not needed unless considered reasonably necessary in the interests of safety.
Coldstream traffic, Warrior, Mike Sierra Golf base (runway) one seven Coldstream.
* these calls are described as "expected" in CAAP 166-1 or "recommended" in AIP ENR 1.1.
